Synomizer / authoring guidance

Clarity, not certification

Return to the editor

The standards-oriented profiles are deterministic authoring aids. They use no transformer, language model or rewriting service. They do not establish full conformity with ASD-STE100 or ISO 24495-1. An unchanged sentence can still need substantial work.

Conversion is not verified release

Version 1.5.0 adds a strict export gate. Block text export until conformity is verified is checked by default in standards modes. Drafts and diagnostic JSON remain available, but copy and text download are disabled while release is blocked. Uncheck the control only to export an explicitly unverified draft. The command-line option --require-conformity returns exit code 3 without creating or truncating a requested output file on a blocked result.

This release has no automatic conformity-approval path. It inventories the 53 ASD-STE100 Issue 9 rule identifiers and four ISO principle-level review areas. The ISO entries are not a complete detailed-guideline checklist. Unimplemented and context-dependent checks stay unverified; absence of findings is not a pass. No arbitrary-text guarantee is made.

Source and draft SHA-256 values bind the normalized text bytes. They are not signatures or approvals. A separate comparison of selected numeric and logical markers can roll back a damaged draft, but marker equality does not prove equivalent meaning.

Additional supported structures

The converter can separate reviewed descriptive clauses and lay out suitable nominal enumerations as vertical lists while retaining the reporting prefix, item order and original conjunction. It does not infer missing actors, silently replace or with and, or expand ambiguous shared-head terms. The Format supported enumerations as lists control can be disabled independently.

Count screening treats a supported vertical-list lead and its items as separate units and groups supported quoted or parenthetical content and common measurements. Supported parenthetical inner units, supplied names/titles and numeric step markers are screened. Complex groups, unreviewed names, layouts and remaining counting requirements still need review. This is not the complete section 8 counting method. The displayed raw word total and grouped sentence-length count have different purposes.

Choose the right task

Three variations is the existing synonym and sentence-variation workflow. ASD-STE100 aid and plain-language aid instead make directional clarity edits and return one consistent draft. Combined gives the same conservative editing path with both sets of review prompts. These profiles do not make terminology more varied merely to create alternatives.

Check only does not rewrite the text; it returns findings and screening metrics. Line endings are normalized to LF, as elsewhere in Synomizer. When unverified draft export is explicitly enabled, copy and text download contain the text only. JSON exports also contain the selected settings, supplied vocabulary, edits and review report.

What is automated

The engine can shorten a small set of wordy constructions, expand unambiguous contractions, change supported simple-past passive clauses when the source explicitly states the actor, and separate supported independent clauses. Conditions, negation, modal obligations and unclear structures block the corresponding structural edits. Quoted text and user-protected terms remain protected according to the selected controls. The original is never replaced.

The report screens sentence length, possible passive constructions, selected tense and -ing patterns, unresolved contractions and vocabulary spellings. A possible passive or an -ing suffix is a review cue, not proof of a violation. The algorithm does not automatically generate instructions, infer missing actors, delete qualifications or decide the intended meaning of a technical term.

What the scope safeguards mean

Supported direct statements such as The panel is clean; the surface is dry. can be separated. Reported or uncertain statements such as The operator has said the valve is open, and the light is on. stay attached. A CLARITY-SCOPE finding asks the author to resolve the scope instead of turning a report into an independently asserted fact. These are limited grammatical patterns, not general semantic verification.

Tense findings also cover supported irregular forms and intervening words, such as has already written and has not removed. They are review cues: the tool does not delete auxiliaries, completed-action meaning or negation to force a simpler tense.

ASD-STE100: Issue 9, 15 January 2025

The correct standard name is ASD-STE100. Its controlled vocabulary depends on approved meanings, grammatical roles, permitted forms and appropriate technical terminology. Merely substituting a common synonym cannot verify these requirements.

The application uses screening targets of 20 words for procedures and 25 for descriptions or notes. Its token counts are estimates, not the complete section 8 counting method. That method treats, among other things, measurements, quoted material, parentheses, names, titles and list structure specially. Review these cases before deciding whether a sentence exceeds the applicable limit.

No official dictionary is bundled. An optional user-authorized TSV can provide terminology and general spellings. The application does not verify that the supplied table is an authorized, complete STE dictionary. It does not resolve a word's approved sense or part of speech merely because its spelling appears in that table. Imported technical terms, names and titles are protected, not automatically certified. Complete review also needs the current rules for procedural content, safety instructions, noun groups, spelling directives, paragraph structure and terminology consistency.

ASD describes software as an aid to authors and does not endorse or certify STE tools. Synomizer is not affiliated with or approved by ASD.

ISO 24495-1:2023

Plain-language work begins with readers and their task. The four review areas are relevance, findability, understanding and usability. The editor records audience and purpose, then prompts the author to review content, organization, terminology, presentation and reader evaluation. Entering context is not equivalent to testing the document with readers.

ISO 24495-1 does not prescribe the application's 25-word screening target. The standard is not a synonym dictionary or a fixed readability-score threshold. The application does not evaluate whether readers can find, understand and use the information. That requires an authoring and evaluation process beyond a text transformation.

Vocabulary TSV format

A UTF-8 byte-order mark at the start of a file is accepted. Use four tab-separated columns without a header: term, part of speech, intended meaning, category. A line beginning with # is a comment. Parts of speech are noun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, pronoun, article or other. Categories are general, technical-noun, technical-verb, name or title. A technical category must agree with its noun or verb part of speech.

The limit is 5,000 entries in 1,000,000 UTF-8 bytes, with at most 200 bytes per term and 500 per meaning. Use only data you are authorized to use. Neither the ASD standard nor the ISO document is distributed under this project's Apache-2.0 licence. The project's clarity rules and examples are independently written, not copies of a standard dictionary.

Bonsai 2 and WebGPU: researched, not embedded

Bonsai 2 is still a transformer-based model. Its publisher lists a 5.95 GB PTQ1_0 language-weight file; this is not a guarantee that a 6 GB GPU or browser can run it. Inference also needs working memory, runtime support and browser GPU capabilities. The publisher's native packs require their compatible runtime rather than an arbitrary GGUF loader.

A community WebGPU demo exists for Ternary-Bonsai-2-27B. This release does not load that model, embed that external page or send your text to it. The link below leaves Synomizer. Model downloads and any caching or data handling on that site are separate from this editor.

A future model-assisted mode would need an explicit opt-in download, a pinned compatible runtime and weights, bounded context and output sizes, cancellation, device-loss recovery and a delete-cache control. Its proposals should pass through the deterministic checker and human review. A model cannot certify conformance or automatically establish scientific equivalence.
